# Homogenize cells in a glass Dounce grinder

A 7 mL glass Dounce tissue grinder stands in an aluminium cooling block on a lab jack, with its tight pestle
standing in 3.5 mL of cell suspension. The robot pushes the pestle to the bottom and gives it 15 full strokes,
each up until the blue tape ring on the shaft clears the tube's rim and back down, without lifting the head out
of the liquid. On every way up the tight pestle sucks the tube up after it, so the other hand has to hold the
tube down in its well the whole time. Then the robot leaves the pestle on the bottom and lets go.

Both arms work at the same time and both decide the result: the right hand's strokes count only while the left
hand keeps the tube seated, and the left hand's job exists only because of the right hand's strokes. Grips,
strokes and the hold-down are finger contact on true-size glass. The liquid is the one stated rule: a drag on
the pestle in proportion to its speed in the tube, with the equal and opposite pull on the tube, capped where
the up-stroke's suction would boil the sample (below).

Scores (run 1, on the final scene and scorer): scripted demonstration 1.0 in 1,451 steps, 15 good strokes of
15, the tube never lifted more than 0.27 mm and held by the left hand through every way up; Dream-1 0.0 (3,000
steps: it drew the pestle up with one hand and the unheld tube came out of its well). Details under "Simulator
run".

## The liquid: what is computed, what is a rule, what can be measured

The 0.025 mm film between head and bore cannot be resolved by contact, and PhysX has no fluid. The task
therefore states one rule (`layout.fluid_force`), applied between the two glass bodies as a PhysX D6 joint with
**every axis free** (it holds nothing in place: the head is kept in the bore by contact with the bore's 16
collision staves, with 0.65 mm of play) and one damping drive along the tube's axis:

    force on the pestle along the tube = -c x (pestle speed relative to the tube) x (share of the head under the surface)
    clipped to +/- 8.6 N; the tube gets the opposite force.

- **The drag coefficient c.** For a tight sphere moving along a tube, lubrication theory (Bungay and Brenner,
  J. Fluid Mech. 1973) gives F = 6 pi mu a U x 9 pi / (4 sqrt 2) x (h / a)^-2.5, with a the head's radius and h
  the radial gap. With PBS plus 5 % glycerol at 4 C (mu about 1.8 mPa s; water is 1.57 mPa s at 4 C) and the
  maker's clearance read as diametral 0.025 to 0.075 mm (radial 0.0125 to 0.0375 mm), c runs from about 3,200
  down to 200 N s/m; read as radial 0.05 mm, 100 N s/m. The task uses **250 N s/m**, at the loose end of that
  range. Real heads are not perfect spheres on the axis (an off-centre head drags much less), so the theory's
  upper end is an overestimate; a hand would need over 50 N to stroke at 18 mm/s there, and people do not.
- **The cap, 8.6 N.** On the way up the head leaves a low pressure under it; it cannot fall below the liquid's
  vapour pressure, so the most the liquid can pull is (101.3 - 0.8 kPa) x the head's 85.8 mm2 = 8.6 N, whatever
  the speed. At the authored c = 250 that is reached at 34.5 mm/s; past it the sample boils and foams. The scorer's foam rule
  (35 mm/s for 0.25 s) is that speed. The same cap is applied on the way down, where it binds only above that
  speed.
- **The surface.** The surface height is computed from the 3.5 mL, the bore, the head's volume and the shaft's
  area (`layout.surface_height`); the head's top is 17 mm under it at the top of the scored stroke band and 2 mm
  under it when the red ring reaches the rim.
- **What PhysX applied (measured).** The joint's drive is authored at 250 N s/m, but in the run the free
  pestle, with no hand near, sank at 3.79 mm/s under its own 0.204 N (`sink_check`, ticks 10 to 60), where
  250 N s/m predicts 0.82 mm/s: the drag PhysX actually applied was about **54 N s/m**, a fifth of the authored
  value and below the lubrication estimate. The scene is kept as run, and the numbers below are the measured
  ones. At the demonstration's 17 to 20 mm/s the pull is therefore about 1 N: well within the arm's ~15 N and
  the grips' friction (12 N per finger on the tube, about 12 N of hold at 0.5 friction; 20 N per finger on the
  handle, about 24 N), and still 7 to 8 times the 0.12 N the tube with its sample weighs. A real tight pestle
  drags harder, so the simulation errs on the easy side for the stroking hand; the hold-down is needed either
  way (next section). The cause of the shortfall in the drive was not isolated; authoring about 1,150 N s/m
  would give the intended 250.
- **Measurable bounds on a bench.** (1) Hang a 200 g weight from the handle and time the head's fall through
  20 mm: c = 1.96 N / speed (250 N s/m: 7.8 mm/s, 2.6 s). (2) Stroke up faster and faster: bubbles appear under
  the head at the cap; c is then 8.6 N / that speed. (3) With a spring scale on the handle, the force at a
  steady 18 mm/s. Whoever builds the bench should do (1) and record the result in `layout.py`.

## Why the second hand is needed (checked, and scored)

- On every way up the tube is pulled up with about 1 N in the simulator (at most 8.6 N) against its own 0.12 N;
  its well holds it only from below. **In the simulator, Dream-1's run shows it:** its left gripper took the
  pestle by the handle, pushed it down and drew it up while no arm touched the tube, and the tube rode up with
  the pestle (13 mm by tick 324 while the head rose only 0.5 mm in the bore); over 723 control steps with the
  tube lifted more than 3 mm no arm was touching it, the head pressed on the bore with under 0.3 N (so the lift
  came through the liquid law, not a jam), and the tube finally rose 54.7 mm, out of its well: latched 0. In the
  stand-in world (`tools/kinematic_world.py`, the glass along the tube's axis with the
  same law, implicit in time) the control run `variant="no_hold"` strokes the pestle with the left hand away:
  the tube rides up with the pestle on the first way up, rises out of its 25 mm well and falls over:
  score 0 ("tube pulled out of its well"/"tube tipped over"). The same control can be run in the simulator with
  `DEMO_VARIANT=no_hold`; it was not part of run 1.
- Creeping the pestle up so slowly that the pull stays under the tube's weight would take about 2 mm/s at the
  measured drag, 11 s for one way up; a stroke counts only if each way takes at most 4 s (at least 5.5 mm/s),
  which forces about 0.3 N on the tube, over twice its weight (at the authored drag, over 1.4 N).
- Lifting tube and pestle out together, or holding the tube anywhere but seated, is caught by the seat check:
  a stroke counts only while the tube's bottom stays within 3 mm of the well's floor and the tube within 5
  degrees of upright, from leaving the bottom to coming back to it.
- The demonstration's left hand presses the tube onto the well's floor (commanded 8 mm below where it gripped)
  and presses harder as soon as the tube rises. In the run its two fingertips pressed at least 8.9 N each on
  every control step of every way up, and the tube never rose more than 0.27 mm.

## Scoring

The scorer is `rdb_dounce/grading.py`; `task.json` states the same rules in words. Every quantity is visible in
the side view: which tape ring is at the tube's rim gives the head's depth (black ring: head 5 mm off the
bottom; blue: 27 mm up; red: head's top 2 mm under the surface), the white line on the tube against the block's
top gives the seat, and the video's clock gives the times.

- **Stroke**: from down (black ring below the rim) to up (blue ring above the rim) and back down. The first push
  down from the start is not a stroke.
- **Good stroke**: tube seated and red ring below the rim throughout, and each way took at most 4 s.
- **0.2 / 0.4 / 0.6** for 5 / 10 / 15 good strokes.
- **0.8**: 15 good strokes, at most 16 strokes in all, never faster than 35 mm/s for 0.25 s (foam) and the red
  ring never above the rim.
- **1.0**: then the pestle left on the bottom, the tube seated, both fingertip centres at least 30 mm from the
  glass, for 1 s.
- **0, latched**: the sample spilled (tube tipped past 30 degrees or lifted 25 mm, out of its well).

Outcomes: idle robot 0; strokes with the tube unheld 0 (it comes out); 14 good strokes 0.4; strokes so fast that
the sample foams 0.6; 17 strokes 0.6; 15 good strokes still holding 0.8; done and let go 1.0.

### Scoring a real trial

Film the side view at the tube's height. Count, frame by frame, the cycles in which the black ring goes below
the rim, the blue ring goes above it and the black ring comes back below; for each, check that the white line
never rose 3 mm above the block's top, that the red ring never showed above the rim, and that each way took at
most 4 s. Note any foam layer and any stroke faster than 35 mm/s (more than 1.75 mm between frames at 20 frames
per second). At the end, check the black ring is below the rim, the tube seated and the hands away.

## Simulation model

- Tube: one rigid body, 12.4 g (glass plus sample), 16 convex wedge hulls (inner faces on the 10.5 mm bore,
  outer faces on the 13 mm outside, two flats turned to face the left hand's pads) and a 16-sided bottom.
- Pestle: one rigid body, 20.8 g: head capsule 9.2 mm across (real 10.45: 0.65 mm of radial play, so the head
  cannot jam on contact offsets), shaft cylinder 6 mm, handle cylinder 14 mm. Contact offset 1 mm, rest
  offset 0, 32 position iterations.
- Liquid: the D6 joint above. Its damping is set each control step from the head's depth (authored 250 N s/m
  throughout a correct trial; about 54 N s/m measured as applied) and applied by PhysX implicitly at every
  240 Hz step.
- Cooling block and jack: static boxes; the block's top layer is four boxes round the well in use leaving a
  14 mm square hole down to the well's floor (drawn round, 14 mm).
- Display only, no collision: the glass surfaces, the sample (its height follows the computed surface), the
  tape rings, the line on the tube, the wells, the jack's scissors, screw and knob, the block's label.

## Run and score a trial

Runtime: the RoboCurve YAM runtime with Python 3.12, Isaac Lab-Arena 0.3.1 on Isaac Sim 6.1 (Linux, NVIDIA RTX
GPU), Inspect Robots 1.0.0, inspect-robots-yam 0.38.0 and yam-arena 0.1.0
([yam-isaac-lab-arena](https://github.com/kairosiann/yam-isaac-lab-arena)). Install this folder into that
environment with `uv pip install --no-deps .`. For the Dream-1 run, put a Dream Labs key where the Dream client
looks for it (`~/.dreamlabs/credentials`); no key is stored here.

```sh
python -m rdb_dounce.run scripted --out out/scripted                    # the scripted demonstration
python -m rdb_dounce.run dream --out out/dream                          # one Dream-1 trial
DEMO_VARIANT=no_hold python -m rdb_dounce.run scripted --out out/no-hold   # control: must score 0
DEMO_VARIANT=fast python -m rdb_dounce.run scripted --out out/fast         # control: foams, at most 0.6
```

Each command runs one trial through Inspect Robots with the scorer `dounce_score`, seed 0, zero object jitter
and a limit of 3,000 actions at 20 Hz (150 simulated seconds). The scene is fixed, so there are no other seeds.
`run.py scripted` exits 0 only if the score is 1.0. Each run writes `RESULT.json` (also when the trial fails
with an error), `media/setup.png` (the review camera), `media/setup_top_cam.png`, `media/top_cam.mp4` (the
overhead policy camera, 320 x 176), `media/side_cam.mp4` (a review-only camera level with the tube in front of
the block, 1280 x 704, never given to a policy) and `media/grader-trace.jsonl`. Policies receive 320 x 176 RGB
from the overhead and the two wrist cameras plus joint and gripper positions, and nothing else. Measured on one
L40S data-centre GPU: the scripted demonstration took 343 s of wall time for its 1,451 steps; the Dream-1 trial
took 747 s for the full 3,000 steps with 87 requests to the model. A per-trial timeout of 1,500 s is ample.

What the policy cameras see (pinhole projection into the calibrated overhead camera, confirmed on the rendered
frames of run 1): the whole set-up is in view from the first frame, near the
image's centre: the jack and block about 20 pixels wide, the tube a glass column about 3 pixels wide from row 81
(the well) to row 73 (the rim), the pestle's shaft and handle up to row 56. The tape rings are at the limit of
what that view resolves; the wrist cameras see them closely once a hand is at the glass.

## The scripted demonstration

`scripted.py`. It reads the robot's joint and gripper readings and, as the review rules allow for a scripted
demonstration, simulator state: the tube's and the pestle's poses and the scorer's running stroke count. It
changes nothing in the simulation.

1. **Reach and grip, both arms at once.** Left hand level, fingers pointing 60 degrees right of straight ahead,
   pads on two flats of the tube 84 mm above the jack's platform (159 mm above the bench); right hand level,
   fingers 45 degrees left of straight ahead, pads on the middle of the glass handle. Each stops 60 mm short,
   corrects for sag from its joint readings, goes in, re-reading the parts' poses, and closes: about 12 N a
   finger on the tube, 20 N on the handle. Each grip is checked by the measured opening and retried once.
2. **Press.** The left hand is commanded 8 mm below where it gripped, pressing the tube onto the well's floor.
3. **First push down** to 2 mm above the bottom (the pestle has sunk a few millimetres while the hands reached).
4. **Strokes** between 2 and 31 mm at 18 mm/s with gentle starts and stops, a 0.15 s pause at each end. The soft
   arm trails a moving pestle by drag / stiffness, so the right hand's height command leads the wanted height by
   a time learned from how far the pestle trailed on the last pass in that direction, plus a slow offset for what
   is left at the ends; nothing integrates fast, so the loop cannot ring or wind up against the bottom. The
   handle is kept over the tube's axis within 4 mm. All the while the left hand presses harder (by 0.8 of the
   rise) as soon as the tube rises, and eases off if its fingers start to slide down the tube. If the tube rose
   more than 1.5 mm on a way up, later strokes are 15 % slower. Strokes go on until the scorer counts 15 good
   ones (at most 16 strokes).
5. **Let go.** With the head on the bottom the right hand opens and backs out 70 mm along its fingers; the left
   hand eases its press, opens and backs out; both arms go to ready.

## Dry runs and tests

```sh
YAM_ARENA=<yam-isaac-lab-arena checkout> python -m pytest -q tests
python tools/dryrun_demo.py --yam-arena <yam-isaac-lab-arena checkout> [--clearance <folder with clearance.py>]
```

The tests need NumPy and pytest (SciPy and yam-arena for the demonstration, which is skipped without them). They
cover every scoring tier and the cheap strategies (first push not a stroke, short strokes, slow creeping strokes,
tube lifted during the strokes, slight lift inside the seat, head out of the liquid, foaming speed, too many
strokes, still holding at the end, pestle left up, unreadable grippers, spills), the numbers in `layout.py`
against the product and the physics, the scene file against `layout.py` (two loose glass bodies and their
masses, the fluid joint free on every axis with one damping drive and collision on, hidden glass colliders,
display-only parts without collision, the block leaving the well open), `task.json` and this README against the
code, and the demonstration in the stand-in world.

The stand-in world (`tools/kinematic_world.py`) has no contact physics: the arms follow their commands with a
lag and a joint sag; a hand closed on its part carries it, vertically as a 0.4 kg point on a 600 N/m spring (so
the drag deflects it as the soft real arm is deflected); along the tube's axis the glass obeys the liquid law,
gravity and one-sided contacts (tube on the well's floor, head on the bore's bottom). Local results of
`dryrun_demo.py`:

- nominal: 1.0 in 1,670 steps (84 s); 15 strokes, all good; peak tube lift 0.9 mm; peak pestle speed 21 mm/s;
  peak drag 5.4 N; joints at least 13.5 degrees inside their limits (joint 4 of the left arm); inverse-kinematics
  error 0.1 mm; from the arms' collision hulls, the arms at least 55 mm apart and at least 6 mm from the block.
- more sag, slower arm, liquid 400 N s/m: 1.0 in 1,815 steps (the demonstration slowed itself once the tube
  rose 1.6 mm). No sag, quick arm, liquid 120 N s/m: 1.0 in 1,437 steps.
- controls: left hand never holds the tube: 0 (tube out of its well on the first way up); strokes at 45 mm/s:
  foams, 0; idle robot: 0.

## Recorded source identity

The packaged layout.py is the exact file from the recorded run bundle. Two post-run diagnostic constants (the measured sinking speed and effective damping) were kept in the documentation and tests instead of changing the recorded physics file. The authored damping remains 250 N s/m; the measured effective value is about 54 N s/m.
