# Serve the first slice from a full pie pan

The operator uses a pie server to remove the first wedge of a chilled pumpkin pie. All eight cut portions begin inside a metal pan. The serving has a bottom and a curved outer crust, a filling and a narrow point that needs support over the gap to the dessert plate. The whole server starts outside the pan.

## Physical reset with stocked pies

Stock identical whole, plain, fully baked refrigerated pumpkin pies from one product and lot in their intact tins before the session. A second qualified source is Starseed Bakery's fully baked 9-inch, 22 oz Traditional Pumpkin Pie (about 78 g per eighth). Select a single product for each series and use the same qualification measurements. [Bakery product specifications](https://starseedbakery.com/products/pumpkin-pie)

Qualify one spare from the lot. Its full, unbroken flared rim should have an inside diameter of 220–235 mm, at least 18 mm of rise and a floor inside diameter of 198–213 mm. Look for a plain bottom and outside pastry enclosing set filling. On a cut face measure 3–5 mm of bottom crust and 16–21 mm of filling. Weigh an eighth (75–90 g). An intact slice should sit upright on a server under its crust, with a recognizably separate filling that can slip or break if unsupported. Reject lots with an open side or removable segment in the pan, pre-lifted portions, paper between portions, a frozen core or a crumb base that will not support the slice. Record the exact product, net mass, pan measurements and lot. Nominal product diameter alone does not establish a match.

At reset, take the next **intact chilled pie in its own tin** from the staged stock. Seat the tin in the fixed nonslip holder at the marked position. Press a clean straight knife along the four diameters on an eight-way guide without lifting any portion or running the blade under the crust. Remove the guide. Check the two target cuts, nominally 2.5 mm, at mid-radius. Return the dry server to its rest entirely outside the pan, replace the plate, fit clean, washable gripper covers, and take synchronized overhead and side reset images. Reuse a pie only for non-scored practice. This is a swap-and-four-cuts reset from refrigerated stock. There is no baking or overnight setting between trials. Record the actual swap-and-cut time at the site.

## Recreating the score on a real serving

Record a level side view and an overhead view. Include a ruler at plate height and one beside the pan. Both jaws, the first wedge and the entire server should be visible. Compare the point and both outer ends of the filling to their original positions over the bottom crust. A drop, an empty blade or the filling leaving its shell does not count as a complete lift. Any observable touch by either gripper to the filling or crust of **any of the eight portions** voids the complete attempt, even after the slice is plated. Examine both fingers, the gripper housing and washable covers.

Keep the seven remaining portions intact in the pan. Small shifts as cut faces separate are expected. Mark the tip area (10 mm radius) and both outer ends (95 mm radius, 20 degrees to either side of each sector center) in the settled overhead still; use the visible pastry landmarks and a ruler. A neighbor moving any mark by 24 mm, half the server heel width, or tilting 15 degrees voids the attempt. Before the first supported lift, compare the neighbor-side edge on each target cut at 35 and 80 mm radius with the reset still. If both marks on one edge move away from the target by 7.5 mm, the nominal 2.5 mm slit has gained a full 10 mm access lane. Opening one of those lanes voids the attempt. Watch the cut while the target is still in the pan; removing the target naturally leaves an empty sector.

Any visibly torn, crumbled, flattened or pinned neighboring portion voids the real trial. A light sideways brush between cut faces does not. The seven simulated portions are independent rigid bodies. To conservatively represent pressing into their tops, the simulation rejects a sustained downward contact load from the server or target food at least equal to the neighbor's weight (about 0.81 N) for a quarter second. A short downward strike also voids the trial: over a rolling 50 ms, sum the downward impulse from the actual contacts above the neighbor's own weight. The limit per 83 g sector is 0.055 N s, the momentum of a sector falling through its 22 mm thickness. This defines an excessive mechanical action rather than predicting pastry fracture. The reference never reached one neighbor weight at any sampled tick. A server or served slice bearing down on a neighbor is visible from the side, with overhead video identifying the portion. A judge cannot recover exact newtons from video; if the food or the suspected contact is hidden, obtain the second view before awarding credit. No local crack or pressure threshold is advertised as a measured property of real custard.

| Credit | Observable event |
| ---: | --- |
| 0.25 | The server supports the whole first slice. The entire bottom crust clears the pan lip by 5 mm for at least 0.25 seconds. Both layers are upright within 15 degrees and their three corresponding landmarks remain within 9 mm. |
| 0.50 | Following the lift, the loaded server carries the entire outlined slice 5 mm past the outer flange on the plate side for at least 0.5 seconds. The same alignment and upright limits apply. |
| 0.75 | Lower the server until plate and blade briefly support the whole aligned serving together. The plate then supports the bottom crust, the entire serving falls within the plate edge, and the aligned layers are upright within 7 degrees for at least 0.25 seconds at the end. |
| 1.00 | The plated condition holds throughout the final second, and the edge of the blade stays at least 5 mm clear of the projected outline of both layers. The overhead view resolves a blade nose that still protrudes under the serving. |

The real handoff requires the blade and plate to carry the slice together. A physics engine can stop reporting forces after an object falls asleep. After the measured handoff the grader also recognizes the resting underside at the plate plane. Six bottom-crust points, including its tip and the rear arc, must all lie within 0.35 mm of the plate surface. This tolerance belongs to the numerical contact test. On a real plate, watch the side view for daylight and the overhead view for a tip extending past the rim. A lifted heel, a raised point or an intact slice left hovering fails the flat-rest check.

Inspect the two recordings frame by frame, including the last second. For an insertion control, angle the blade into the gap inside the outer crust and lift only the filling. A second control inserts the server shallowly under the heel. These trials test whether the original base, outer pastry and filling actually travel together. For a release control, leave part of the blade under the fully plated wedge.

## Physical scope

A rigid stainless server, complete flared pan wall, raised pastry and all eight cut portions participate in contact. The original target crust and filling can translate, rotate, separate and fall. Other portions approximate firm cut solids. Friction values are estimates. Within-layer cracking, crumbs, adhesion to the pan, local flexing of the blade and deformation of warm or loose filling lie outside this model. Use fresh, firmly set slices for physical repetitions. Count a visibly broken or missing target piece in the real trial as an incomplete serving and document that additional failure mode.
