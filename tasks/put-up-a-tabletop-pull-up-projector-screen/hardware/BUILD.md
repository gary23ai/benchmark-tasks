# Tabletop screen fabrication and commissioning

Dimensions are nominal millimetres. The spring forces are calculated targets; enter your measured values on the commissioning sheet before running a physical trial.

Open [screen-fixture.dxf](screen-fixture.dxf) in a metric CAD viewer. Model space contains full-size flat patterns, three guide elevations, the closed/open support and the lid cutouts. ## Datums and mechanism

Use local coordinates X, Y and Z throughout. Add 440 to X to obtain the task's world x. World y = Y. The tabletop is Z=0. Viewed from the arms, positive Y is to the left. The sheet exits at X=0. The support axis is (0,245). Heights in the following table are top or bearing surfaces, not centre-to-centre dimensions.

| Feature | Closed | Hung |
|---|---:|---:|
| Housing lid / bottom of bar in reset | 85 | 85 (fixed lid) |
| Bar underside / top | 85 / 101 | 390 / 406 |
| Tab underside / top | 147 / 151 | 452 / 456 |
| Post shoulder / pin tip | 132 / 140 | 452 / 460 |

Two separate guides fit the box. A wide X linkage behind the cloth keeps the top bar horizontal under the off-centre load. The shorter ten-cell linkage pulls the support cap straight up and locks at its base. Concentric sleeves at the support axis telescope as the cap rises. The cap can return from any intermediate height. The separate top bar can rise 345 from rest to clear the pin.

## Stock, profiles and fasteners

Use 6061-T6 aluminium for links, tubes and machined blocks, steel shoulder screws or dowels for pivots and acetal for sliders. Deburr sheet edges, break pin tips 0.5 and line both sides of the cloth opening with smooth tape. Default cut tolerance ±0.5, hole centres ±0.15, mating diameters and slots as given. Drill link stacks together in a jig. Shim paired plates to parallel within 0.3 over their length. Use stainless machine screws with prevailing-torque nuts or removable threadlocker on tapped mounts. Moving pivots must turn on an unthreaded shank; use 0.2–0.3 axial running clearance and thin washers between moving plates.

## Mount and time the mechanisms

1. **Lay out the housing.** Mark the four board corners X=±40, Y=±300. The lid has a 6-wide exit slot at X=0, Y=-208..208, a rear guide slot X=14..30, Y=-245..213, and a mast opening X=-29..29, Y=214..298. Merge overlapping cuts. Use a removable perimeter frame with individual slot lips, reinforced underneath outside the swept envelopes. Through-cut short local reliefs for fastener heads; all edges beside moving members need ≥1 clearance. Provide a removable, countersunk centre island or split the mast-end lid if cuts overlap. Keep the narrow central bar seat at X=±5..9, Y=-200..200. Place roller bearing brackets wholly toward negative X, ending at X=-4, with a shallow bridge over the lower return cord if necessary. Check that fixed brackets, side angles and lid screws clear moving parts before installing the lid.
2. **Wind the roller.** Centre the cloth and fasten the bar to its free edge with its underside on the lid in reset. At maximum rise, at least 122 mm remains wrapped, allowing 345 travel, 33 free tangent length inside the case, 20 bond and 15 top clamp out of 535. Including the closed wraps, maximum outside radius is about 15.2 (core 14; sheet thickness ≤0.20). Set the drum so taking fabric *off* the tube takes cord *onto* the drum. Use at least one dead turn on the drum in reset. A Ø14 horizontal-plane idler (vertical pin) at Y=-225 redirects the cord from the drum's upper tangent Z=61.4 into the +Y direction at X=-34. Allow the working wraps to traverse the drum face without rubbing the flange.
3. **Route the roller springs entirely on the screen side of the mast.** Anchor both fixed ends near Y=-292, axes X=-30, Z=30 and 73. Use an adjustable clevis to set each spring 75 above its measured free hook-to-hook length in reset. Join the free ends to an equaliser at the same Y with two bent tabs; the central 1.5-thick web lies at X=-34 for Z=39..64, clearing the fully wound roller's most negative X of about -30.2. The drum's lead runs along X=-34, Z=61.4 to a turnaround pulley at Y=208 with axle parallel to X, groove radius 5.5, width ≤3.5. It returns toward negative Y at X=-34, Z=50.4 and ties to the equaliser web. Place a smooth eye at the equaliser's upper crossing with the outbound line (Ø3 clear, or a small fork around it), so the web can pass without catching the running cord. All roller spring hardware fits below Y=216, ahead of the mast links. At 345 payout cord take-up is at most 345×9.4/14 = 232; with the longest allowed spring, the equaliser finishes by Y=195. Bend the end brackets to keep each hook axially aligned and ≥1 from the housing and roll. Check the negative-X bearing plate relief at Y=-215 for both springs and strands. At the sheet, predicted tension is the sum of the two spring tensions ×9.4 / effective sheet radius, about 4.8–5.2 N near reset and 6.8–7.1 N after lifting 305.
4. **Fit the separate anti-tilt guide.** The rear guide is one crossed pair, with the links in adjacent planes centred at X=20 and 24. Both fixed pivot centres are Y=-236. Bottom pivot Z=18; upper pivot Z=88 in reset (3 above the bar underside), on a rigid rear bracket bolted to the bar. Make this bracket from 4-thick angle stock spanning Y=-241..-185; bolt it to two M4 tapped holes on the bar rear face, Y=-203 and -190. The guide track ends at or before Y=204 throughout its stroke, outside the support linkage. Bottom track fixed to base; upper track fastened and shimmed parallel to the bar, likewise below its top surface at reset. In each track, Y increases from the fixed pivot toward the free slider. Orient the DXF pattern with its centreline horizontal; place the slot start centre at Y=-236+6. Join lower fixed to upper sliding and lower sliding to upper fixed through the midpoint pivot. For a bar rise b, spacing between fixed and sliding ends = sqrt(435² − (70+b)²); it moves from 429.3 to 130.4. The upper/lower tracks must accommodate 6..431 from fixed. Retain each roller with a washer larger than the slot. At each bracket, offset the pivot to the correct link plane with spacers so the joint never binds. Set an adjustable stop screw in the lower track at the slider position for 345 rise. This linkage carries the moment from supporting the bar at one end. Its joints and friction are physical approximations of the simulation's vertical bar slide.
5. **Build the mast.** At each of X=-23 and +23 make a ten-cell lazy-tong stack; separate the two link layers at each plane by a shim. Bolt the crossing holes at their centres. Each cell shares its two upper terminal pivots with the lower terminals of the cell above; the outermost terminals connect to the fixed pin and the roller of each track. Fixed pivots (bottom and top) have Y=269; the slots run toward *decreasing* Y, allowing roller centres from 8..58 from that pivot. Bottom pivots Z=18, top pivots 10 below the shoulder (122 in reset). For shoulder travel h the stack height is 104+h and shoe spacing W = sqrt(47² − ((104+h)/10)²). Set the two lower outer travel stops to W=45.835 in reset; set stops to the measured 132 shoulder datum. At h=320 W≈20.28: machine the full-extension stops to give an actual shoulder height 452±1. Keep pivots isolated at each side (no cross-shaft through the concentric sleeves). Locate track cheeks just outside the complete link stack, allowing their width and all heads within the lid opening; widen the mast opening locally beside a pivot head where required. Install/telescope the five sleeves from the outer in, installing each stroke stop before the next inner sleeve. The cap is attached to the bridge, and pulling it raises both scissor stacks. With the stack on the final stop, all four tube slots should retain 26±1 overlap. Set the sleeve stop ends 0.2 long if necessary to let the lift stop take first contact.
6. **Make the click and reset.** The locking pin moves horizontally along X through the +X *lower* track's outside cheek into the side of the moving shoe's strike plate. Support the strike plate on a small shoe block riding along the *outside* of the track; a second stud in the slot, 6 toward the decreasing-Y end, prevents the shoe rotating. Both stud centres must stay in the slot at both limits. The moving outside surface of the plate cams the pin in and holds it back until the hole aligns. Clamp the mechanism with the shoulder at 452; transfer-drill the receiving hole through the pin-guide jig at Y=269−W, Z=18 (offset the hole 5 along Y from the roller axle and move the guide by the same amount to clear that axle). Use a flanged lock body that passes through the case side, bolted to the cheek by two M3 screws. Along X in the engaged position: strike plate 30..33, pin guide 33..43, Ø6 stem collar 43..46 inside a Ø6.5 pocket, spring 46..55, hollow rear plug from 55..58. The pin tip stops at X=30.2 when the collar meets the guide. The spring pushes the collar toward the shoe. Guard the protruding release with an open-ended U-channel extending to X=66; the lock housing and release project outside the nominal 80-wide case at this location. Keep the spring covered, with manual pull accessible through a clearance hole in the case's positive-X face. At h=317 the shoe should still be just outside engagement; verify first engagement in the last 3 of rise, and adjust the bracket with slots if necessary. Do not taper the load-bearing length of the pin. Unload the shoulder with a hand, pull the pin out by 3.5 and start lowering, then let the pin ride over the plate. The latch must support the downward load with the spring acting only as its engagement force.
7. **Return the post.** Fasten a small downward cord eye to the solid bridge lug at X=16, Y=270, beside the knob cutout. Lead the cord vertically at X=16, Y=270 to a flanged fixed pulley (groove radius 7, axle parallel to X, centre X=16, Y=263, Z=18). The cord exits its lower tangent toward decreasing Y at Z=11. Route under the sleeve socket through a milled 3-wide × 3-high passage centred at X=16,Z=11. The sleeve ends stay at or above Z=14. Lead this strand around a moving Ø14 groove-diameter sheave with vertical axle, centred at X=9, Z=11; the other strand leads back toward increasing Y at X=2 and terminates at an eye at Y=202, Z=11. Use a 3-thick sheave and a low-profile axle with at least 0.5 clearance over the base. Fasten the spring along X=9 to the base near Y=-290. A small offset clevis lifts the spring centre to Z=16. Set the spring 30 above free length in reset and trim a tail between spring and moving sheave to put that sheave near Y=-100. It travels +160, well short of its fixed eye. A 320 cap pull takes 160 of spring travel, giving a predicted return pull of (2.9+0.024×30)/2 = 1.81 N initially and 3.73 N at full height. Check for return at several intermediate heights before fitting the lid.
8. **Align and finish.** Hold the cap at 452, lift the bar until the tab clears the tip and align the 12 window over the 8 pin; at least 1 clearance per side in the centred assembly. Square the bar on both ends and shim its rear bracket before fixing the track screws. Verify that the shoulder, rather than a bolt head or bridge plate, carries the tab when lowered. The wide guide and the mast operate independently. The visible real guide members, tube diameters and latch differ from the simplified simulation. Set the fixture on the same tape marks and place the two arms and cameras as specified in task.json.

## Physical commissioning sheet

Use a ruler, a 0–20 N hook scale, known small weights, and an end and side view; record date, builder, actual readings and any shim/adjuster changes. Do this before scoring a physical trial.

- With springs uncoupled and bar supported, stroke both mechanisms by hand over their full travel. Confirm the lid, central pin, screen edge and the two linkages clear each other. The rear guide must support the bar level with the roller pulling on the centre and the carrier supported only at the mast end.
- Disconnect the cloth from the bar, or support the bar and guide independently to remove weight and linkage friction from the reading. Measure slow pull-out and slow return tension in the cloth near rest and at 305 payout. Record both; aim for their midpoint within 5±1 N and 7±1 N respectively. Adjust equaliser preload for the first value; check or replace the matched springs if the increase is outside target. Reattach the cloth and test smooth return.
- Unclip the mast spring line from its cap and measure the line tension with the hook scale at 0 and 320 travel; aim for 1.8±0.5 and 3.7±0.7 N. Refit to the cap. The post should retract under its own weight and return spring if released before the last 3 of stroke. Adjust joint friction and preload until it does.
- Measure bar underside 85 in reset and 390±10 seated, shoulder 132 in reset and 452±1 locked, and usable extra travel sufficient to lift the tab over the 460 pin tip. After latching, release both hands for ≥2 s. Check the tab surrounds the pin and a 4 mm gauge cannot pass freely under the seated tab. Inspect both ends of the bar for tilt or guide binding.
- Unseat/reseat three times and operate the reset release with load supported. Reject a build that tips, shifts off its tape marks, jams, or will not remain hung. Use the original real-trial scoring thresholds only after commissioning passes. Keep fingers clear of the scissor stacks; use guarded access for reset and cover the spring channels when running trials.
